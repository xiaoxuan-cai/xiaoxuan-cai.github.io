## Research

My research sits at the intersection of statistical methodology and mental health, with a focus on intensive longitudinal data and mobile health (mHealth) studies. It is motivated by data streams that record human behavior in real time, such as ecological momentary assessment (EMA) and wearable sensors. These data raise distinct statistical problems: strong temporal dependence, non-stationarity, heterogeneity across individuals, and complex missingness. I develop methods that capture these dynamics and support individualized decision-making.

Current methodological work includes

- hierarchical Bayesian state-space models;
- dynamic and individualized treatment effects;
- non-stationary time series and longitudinal data analysis;
- causal inference with time-varying treatments;
- missing data and data integration in intensive longitudinal settings.

Current applied work includes

- precision mental health;
- suicide risk modeling and prevention;
- mobile health and digital phenotyping;
- behavioral and psychiatric data science.

The work is problem-driven. I start from a scientific or clinical question and then develop the statistical tools it requires. Depending on the problem, that may mean Bayesian modeling, frequentist inference, machine learning, or a combination of these.
