I am an Assistant Professor in the [Department of Statistics](https://stat.osu.edu/) at The Ohio State University, which I joined in 2024. I am also affiliated with the [College of Public Health](https://cph.osu.edu/) and the [Translational Data Analytics Institute](https://tdai.osu.edu/).

I received my Ph.D. training in Biostatistics with [Dr. Forrest W. Crawford](http://www.crawfordlab.io/) at the [Yale School of Public Health](https://ysph.yale.edu/). I completed my postdoctoral training with [Dr. Linda Valeri](https://www.lindavaleri.com/) in the [Department of Biostatistics](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/academics/departments/biostatistics) at the Columbia Mailman School of Public Health.

My research sits at the intersection of statistical methodology and mental health, with a focus on intensive longitudinal data and mobile health (mHealth) studies. It is motivated by data streams that record human behavior in real time, such as ecological momentary assessment (EMA) and wearable sensors. These data raise distinct statistical problems, including strong temporal dependence, non-stationarity, heterogeneity across individuals, and complex missingness. I develop methods that capture these dynamics and support individualized decision-making.

### Statistical interests

- Hierarchical Bayesian state-space models
- Dynamic and individualized treatment effects
- Non-stationary time series and longitudinal data analysis
- Causal inference with time-varying treatments
- Missing data and data integration in intensive longitudinal settings

### Applied interests

- Precision mental health
- Suicide risk modeling and prevention
- Mobile health (mHealth) and digital phenotyping
- Behavioral and psychiatric data science

### For students

Students interested in a reading course, a research assistant position, or a dissertation topic are welcome to contact me, especially if you would like to work on:

- Time series analysis
- Latent state and dynamic modeling
- Individualized treatment effect estimation
- Causal inference in intensive longitudinal settings
- Missing data and real-world data analysis
- Statistical methods for mobile and sensor data

My research is problem-driven. I start from a scientific or clinical question and then develop the statistical tools it requires. Depending on the problem, that may mean Bayesian modeling, frequentist inference, machine learning, or a combination of these. The goal is methods that are theoretically sound and useful in high-impact applications.
